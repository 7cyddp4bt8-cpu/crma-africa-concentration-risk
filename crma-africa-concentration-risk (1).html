Critical Raw Materials Act — External Strategy Analysis

The EU has legislated against concentration risk and then systematically built it, without a hedge

The CRMA's African partnership architecture is not a diversification strategy but a dependency substitution — and the Act's own risk framework proves it. This brief maps the evidence across five partnership agreements, seven critical minerals, and twelve analytical sources.

The precedent

Germany did not build this dependency through negligence

Germany built its Russian gas dependency through Wandel durch Handel — a coherent, explicitly argued strategic logic. Deep economic interdependence as a pacifying force. The argument had serious intellectual backing, political consensus across decades, and institutional reinforcement through Nordstream 1 and 2. It was also catastrophically wrong. When it failed, it failed completely — no hedge, no fallback, no buffer that mattered.

The EU is now replicating that exact architecture in critical minerals, with African states, and calling it diversification. Germany's gas dependency had four characteristics that made it catastrophic: no liquid spot market, no stored buffer, no contracted alternatives, and infrastructure lock-in. The EU's African mineral position shares all four.

"The EU has legislated against concentration risk and then systematically built it, without a hedge. The CRMA's African architecture is not a diversification strategy but a dependency substitution — and its own risk framework proves it."

The CRMA's own Article benchmark prohibits more than 65% reliance on any single country for any strategic raw material. Of seven key minerals assessed here, five already breach this threshold. The DRC exercised exactly this leverage in February 2025 with a cobalt export ban, extended twice, cutting 2026 quota volumes to roughly half of normal monthly levels. The architecture failed its own stress test before it was fully built.

De lege ferenda — three proposals for the July 2026 CRMA review

What a hedged architecture looks like

Proposal 01
Apply Article 5 concentration risk methodology formally to external partnerships
Before any bilateral CRM agreement is signed, the Commission must publish a mandatory concentration risk assessment using the same criteria applied to domestic strategic materials. If the partnership deepens single-country exposure beyond 65%, it requires a compensating hedge instrument as a condition of proceeding. The legislation already contains the methodology. Apply it consistently.
Proposal 02
Replace fragmented bilaterals with a single AU-EU Critical Minerals Framework Agreement
One multilateral instrument with all African partners simultaneously eliminates single-country political risk. African states with divergent interests would not coordinate an export restriction uniformly — that divergence is the hedge. The bilateral architecture forfeits this natural diversification in exchange for the appearance of bilateral relationships that each carry full single-country exposure.
Proposal 03
Mandate EIB first-loss co-investment in African processing infrastructure
To receive EU strategic project status, African partnerships must demonstrate a minimum 25% of capital expenditure allocated to in-country processing, with the EIB holding a subordinated equity position — losing money before African partners do if the project underperforms. Shared downside is what aligns incentives. Political agreements do not. This is the skin-in-the-game mechanism that makes the relationship structurally stable rather than politically contingent.
